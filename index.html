Signing should take a minute, not a printer

The traditional way to sign a PDF is absurd when you say it out loud: print the document, find a pen, sign the paper, find a scanner or photograph the page, convert it back to a PDF, and send a file that looks worse than the one you started with. Every step exists only because the signature was treated as ink instead of information. A digital signature image placed precisely on the page skips the entire relay — and the result is cleaner, because the document itself is never degraded by a print-and-scan round trip.

InkSeal gives you three ways to create that signature, because people differ. Drawing with a mouse or finger feels most like signing; typing your name in a handwriting style is neatest when drawing feels wobbly; uploading a prepared signature image is best when you already have one you like — perhaps a transparent PNG you keep for exactly this purpose. Whichever you create, you place it once, on the page and in the position the form intends, and download a finished document.

Placement: where InkSeal beats guesswork

A signature floating in the wrong place looks worse than no signature process at all — it signals carelessness on a document whose whole purpose is care. InkSeal therefore treats placement as a first-class control: choose the page, start from a sensible preset — bottom right is where most forms expect a signature — then nudge horizontally and vertically with sliders and set the width in points. The preview shows the document's first page so you can orient yourself before committing.

Two finishing details matter. The optional date stamp prints today's date beside the signature in small type, as most paper forms intend. And the signature is flattened into the saved PDF: it becomes part of the page content rather than a movable annotation layer, so it cannot be accidentally dragged, detached or lost when the file passes through other software. What you placed is what every reader, on every device, will see.

How to sign a PDF, step by step

Load the document first and glance at the preview to confirm it is the right file and to note where the signature line lives. Create your signature — a firm, unhurried draw usually beats a fast scribble, and the Clear button is free, so take two attempts if the first looks like someone else wrote it. Set the page number if the signature belongs beyond page one, choose the preset closest to the target, and adjust width so the signature sits naturally in its space: roughly the width of the signature line is the right instinct.

Tick the date option if the form has a date field beside the signature, press Sign & Build, and download the signed copy — saved under a new '-signed' name so your unsigned original remains pristine. Open the result once, zoom to the signature, and confirm it sits where you intended. The whole ritual, honestly timed, runs under a minute once your signature style is chosen.

Is a drawn signature legally meaningful?

In most everyday contexts — internal approvals, school and club forms, tenancy paperwork, freelance agreements, delivery acknowledgements — a signature image applied by the signer is accepted exactly as a wet signature is, because what organisations actually need is visible evidence of assent from the right person. Many jurisdictions recognise electronic signatures broadly for ordinary contracts, while reserving stricter requirements for specific instruments such as property deeds, wills and certain notarised documents.

Be clear about what InkSeal is not: it is not a cryptographic digital-signature service with certificates, identity verification and tamper-evident audit trails. When a document demands that level — a regulated filing, a high-value contract where the counterparty specifies a signing platform — use the specified platform. For the ninety-five percent of life that simply asks 'please sign and return', a clean signature properly placed on the PDF is precisely what the sender wants, delivered without the print-scan pantomime.

Privacy: your signature is your identity — keep it local

Of all the artefacts a person uploads to random websites, a clean image of their signature should top the worry list: it is a reusable proof of identity, and copies have obvious misuse potential. InkSeal's architecture answers that concern structurally. The document and the signature are combined by libraries running inside your own browser tab; neither is transmitted anywhere, and both vanish from memory when the tab closes. The signed copy travels only where you send it.

That local processing is also why there are no accounts, no stored signature galleries and no 'your documents' cloud folder you never asked for. You bring the file, you bring the signature, the tool joins them on your machine, and everyone goes home. For a task whose entire output is an attestation of who you are, that discretion is not a feature — it is the point.

Signature craft: small habits, professional results

Draw your signature at the size it will be used: tiny signatures drawn on a big pad shrink into nervous squiggles, so fill the pad generously and let the width setting do the scaling. Sign in one confident motion rather than retracing strokes — retracing reads as forgery practice even when it is only neatness. If your organisation signs many documents, standardise: same signature source, same width, same bottom-right placement, so counterparties recognise your execution block on sight. And preview the signed page at full size once before sending; a signature overlapping the printed name line is the commonest placement fault, and the nudge sliders exist precisely to move it the few points it needs.
